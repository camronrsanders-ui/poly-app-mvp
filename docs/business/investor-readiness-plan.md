# Polycircle — Investor & Acquisition Readiness Plan

> **Priority rule:** This business work is secondary to shipping a stable, secure, testable Polycircle application. It may be expanded during low-risk planning time, but it must not displace engineering work needed for runtime stability, Android/iOS validation, security, or release readiness.

## Purpose

Build the business materials, evidence, metrics, documentation, and strategic story required to give Polycircle credible options to:

1. raise outside investment;
2. build a sustainable independent company;
3. pursue strategic partnerships;
4. entertain acquisition interest from a larger dating, social, LGBTQ+, relationship-health, or community platform if doing so serves the product and its users.

The objective is not simply to imitate an existing dating app. Polycircle should learn from successful category leaders while developing a differentiated relationship platform centered on connection structure, consent, education, safety, and community.

## Founder / business operating mode

When working on business strategy, use a second operating perspective alongside engineering:

- **Product engineer:** protect reliability, security, privacy, and execution quality.
- **Founder / entrepreneur:** build market evidence, positioning, distribution, revenue strategy, partnerships, investor readiness, and long-term enterprise value.

Neither role overrides the other. Business growth must not require unsafe engineering shortcuts, and technical perfection must not prevent disciplined market validation.

## Core business story

### Mission
Help people **love, grow, and connect** with better tools for understanding and navigating modern relationships.

### Product thesis
Most dating products focus primarily on discovering and messaging people. Polycircle can extend across the relationship lifecycle:

- **Meet — Discover**
- **Understand — Circle**
- **Grow — Community Learning & Support**
- **Evolve — Past Connections & Reconnection**

### Strategic differentiation
Potential durable differentiators to validate with users:

- privacy-aware interactive relationship mapping through Circle;
- relationship context rather than treating every connection as an isolated match;
- explicit support for polyamorous/ENM relationship structures;
- consent-aware connection endings and future mutual reconnection;
- education, terminology, safety, and community resources inside the product;
- strong privacy/safety architecture as a product advantage rather than only a compliance requirement;
- an eventual community network effect built around relationships and trusted connections, not just profile inventory.

## Business plan workstreams

### 1. Executive summary
Maintain a concise version answering:
- What is Polycircle?
- Who is it for?
- What painful or underserved problem does it solve?
- Why now?
- What makes it meaningfully different?
- What evidence shows people want it?
- How can it grow?
- How can it make money without compromising the mission?
- What capital/resources are needed and what milestones would that capital unlock?

### 2. Market research
Build a sourced research file covering:
- polyamory/ENM population estimates and limitations of available research;
- LGBTQ+ dating/social-app behavior where relevant;
- dating-app market size, growth, and business models;
- willingness to pay and subscription behavior;
- user pain points with current dating/ENM products;
- regional launch opportunities;
- safety/trust concerns that affect adoption;
- market trends that could make relationship-structure products more relevant.

All investor-facing market numbers must have a source, publication date, methodology note where necessary, and a `lastVerified` date. Avoid inflated TAM claims based on weak assumptions.

### 3. Competitive landscape
Maintain a current comparison of relevant products, potentially including:
- mainstream dating platforms;
- LGBTQ+ dating/social leaders;
- poly/ENM-specific products;
- relationship-management or couple-oriented tools;
- community/social platforms that overlap with Polycircle's future vision.

Compare on:
- target user;
- geographic reach;
- core interaction model;
- relationship-structure support;
- safety/privacy features;
- pricing/monetization;
- network/community features;
- strengths;
- weaknesses/user complaints;
- Polycircle differentiation.

Do not frame the strategy as "be another Grindr." Study category leaders for distribution, retention, monetization, network effects, brand recognition, and public-company/acquisition lessons while maintaining Polycircle's distinct product thesis.

### 4. Customer discovery
Create a repeatable research program:
- founder interviews with poly/ENM users;
- beta-user interviews;
- anonymous surveys where appropriate;
- usability studies for Discover, Circle, safety, and onboarding;
- willingness-to-pay research;
- churn/deletion interviews where consented;
- community expert/advisor interviews.

Track evidence separately from founder assumptions.

### 5. Go-to-market plan
Develop launch hypotheses for:
- Boston / New England pilot community;
- LGBTQ+ and poly/ENM community organizations;
- local events and community partnerships;
- ambassadors/community leaders;
- creator/educator partnerships;
- referral loops;
- app-store optimization;
- earned media / founder story;
- carefully managed paid acquisition only after retention is understood.

The first objective should be **density in selected communities**, not superficial nationwide download numbers.

### 6. Monetization strategy
Evaluate options without locking the roadmap prematurely:

Potential paid convenience/value features:
- advanced discovery/filter controls;
- optional premium visibility/discovery tools that preserve user control;
- enhanced organization/customization of Circle;
- see-who-liked-you or similar convenience functionality;
- optional premium communication/community utilities;
- event/community-related offerings;
- future partner services where aligned with user interests.

Principles:
- blocking, reporting, consent controls, account safety, and essential crisis/help resources remain free;
- do not design monetization around intentionally frustrating free users;
- avoid business incentives that reward unsafe engagement or exposure of sensitive relationship data;
- validate pricing experimentally rather than assuming competitor pricing automatically transfers.

### 7. Metrics and traction
Prepare instrumentation and reporting for investor-quality metrics once privacy review permits collection.

Potential product metrics:
- verified/active users;
- DAU / WAU / MAU;
- D1 / D7 / D30 retention;
- onboarding completion;
- Discover-to-profile-view rate;
- Like/Pass behavior;
- mutual connection rate;
- conversations started per connection;
- meaningful conversation/response rate;
- Circle adoption and repeat use;
- safety-report/block rates and resolution quality;
- reconnection usage after launch;
- resource-hub engagement using privacy-safe aggregation;
- subscription conversion and churn if monetization launches.

Potential business metrics:
- CAC;
- organic/referral share;
- LTV;
- ARPU / ARPPU;
- gross margin;
- burn/runway;
- market-level user density;
- cohort retention;
- payback period.

Do not collect sensitive metrics merely because investors may ask for them. Data collection must remain proportional and privacy-reviewed.

### 8. Financial model
Build a conservative 3–5 year model with explicit assumptions for:
- users and active-user growth;
- paid conversion;
- subscription/pricing scenarios;
- app-store/platform fees;
- Firebase/cloud infrastructure;
- moderation/safety operations;
- engineering/design/product staffing;
- legal/compliance/accounting;
- insurance;
- marketing/community acquisition;
- customer support;
- administrative overhead;
- runway and fundraising requirements.

Create base, downside, and upside cases. Keep assumptions editable and sourced where external benchmarks are used.

### 9. Funding strategy
Prepare for multiple paths:
- bootstrapped validation;
- grants or mission-aligned programs where available;
- angel/pre-seed funding;
- accelerator applications;
- seed institutional capital after meaningful traction;
- strategic investment/partnership.

For each raise, define:
- amount sought;
- valuation/capital structure assumptions;
- milestone use of funds;
- expected runway;
- specific evidence required before approaching investors.

Avoid fundraising merely for prestige. Raise when capital can accelerate a validated opportunity or important operational requirement.

### 10. Acquisition / buyer readiness
Build the company so strategic acquisition is an **option**, not the only exit plan.

Potential buyer categories to research over time:
- dating/social portfolio companies;
- LGBTQ+ social/dating platforms;
- mainstream dating groups;
- relationship-health/wellness companies;
- community/event platforms;
- other social-network businesses seeking differentiated relationship graph/community technology.

Buyer-readiness requirements:
- clean IP ownership;
- organized source control and documentation;
- clear founder/contractor invention assignments;
- privacy/compliance records;
- security posture and incident history;
- reliable product metrics;
- clean financial books;
- cap table/documentation;
- trademark/brand records;
- vendor contracts;
- data processing/subprocessor inventory;
- defensible product/technical differentiation;
- evidence of retention and community loyalty.

Do not optimize prematurely for sale at the expense of user trust. A product with strong trust, retention, community density, and clean operations is also a more attractive acquisition target.

### 11. Legal, corporate, and IP readiness
Before outside capital or a serious buyer process, obtain qualified professional advice and organize:
- company formation/entity structure;
- founder equity and vesting decisions;
- cap table;
- invention/IP assignments;
- trademarks/name clearance;
- open-source license inventory;
- Terms of Service / Privacy Policy;
- app-store compliance;
- data-processing agreements;
- contractor/employee agreements;
- tax/accounting setup;
- insurance appropriate to the business;
- fundraising securities-law compliance.

This document is planning material and not legal, tax, accounting, or securities advice.

### 12. Data room
Create an organized investor/acquisition data-room structure when appropriate:

- `01 Company & Corporate`
- `02 Product & Roadmap`
- `03 Technology & Security`
- `04 Market & Competition`
- `05 Users & Traction`
- `06 Financials`
- `07 Legal & Compliance`
- `08 Team`
- `09 Partnerships`
- `10 Fundraising / Transaction Materials`

Never place production secrets, raw private user data, or unnecessary personal data in an investor data room.

## Evidence levels

Use labels in business materials so assumptions are not confused with facts:

- **Vision:** founder/product direction.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible idea that needs validation.
- **Evidence:** supported by user research or reliable external data.
- **Traction:** demonstrated by actual Polycircle usage/business metrics.
- **Decision:** approved operating/product choice.

## Current phase

**Stage: Pre-launch / product validation preparation.**

Current business priority is to preserve the story, research structure, and diligence discipline while engineering makes the app reliably testable. Once closed-alpha testing begins, the highest-value business work shifts toward customer discovery and collecting privacy-safe evidence of retention, connection quality, Circle value, and willingness to pay.

## Success definition

Polycircle should aim to become a trusted, recognizable relationship platform with a loyal community and a sustainable business. Fundraising, profitability, strategic partnership, or acquisition are possible outcomes—not the mission themselves. The strongest negotiating position comes from building a product people genuinely value, a community that stays, and a company whose technology, finances, security, and records can withstand professional diligence.
